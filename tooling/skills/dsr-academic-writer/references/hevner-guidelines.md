# Hevner et al. Guidelines — Expanded Reference

## 1. Design as an Artifact
The result of DSR is a purposeful IT artifact. Four types:
- **Constructs**: vocabulary and symbols
- **Models**: abstractions and representations
- **Methods**: algorithms and practices
- **Instantiations**: implemented and working systems

A POC is almost always an instantiation. Extract higher-level knowledge (principles, models) from it.

## 2. Problem Relevance
Address important business or organizational problems. Relevance is judged by practitioners and academics. Ground the problem in both literature and real-world evidence.

## 3. Design Evaluation
Utility, quality, and efficacy must be demonstrated. Evaluation methods (Hevner taxonomy):

**Observational**
- Case study
- Field study

**Analytical**
- Static analysis
- Architecture analysis
- Optimization
- Dynamic analysis

**Experimental**
- Controlled experiment
- Simulation

**Testing**
- Functional testing
- Structural testing

**Descriptive**
- Informed argument
- Scenarios

Choose methods that match the maturity of the artifact and the strength of the claims.

## 4. Research Contributions
Contributions can be:
- The design artifact itself
- Foundations (constructs, models, methods, design principles)
- Methodologies

Gregor & Hevner (2013) contribution levels:
- Level 1: Situated implementation (specific instantiation)
- Level 2: Nascent design theory (design principles, constructs, methods)
- Level 3: Well-developed design theory

Most good DSR papers after a POC aim for solid Level 2.

## 5. Research Rigor
Apply rigorous methods in both construction and evaluation. Document the process so others can understand the search and the evidence.

## 6. Design as a Search Process
Design is iterative. Show the means (tools, techniques), ends (goals), and laws (constraints) of the environment. Document alternatives considered and why they were rejected.

## 7. Communication of Research
Present to two audiences:
- Technology-oriented: sufficient detail to implement or extend
- Management-oriented: sufficient detail to decide on adoption and organizational impact
