# Common Pitfalls That Lead to Rejection

## 1. Pure Engineering / "We Built a System"
- Problem: Paper describes the POC but extracts no generalizable design knowledge.
- Fix: Elevate from instantiation to design principles or a method. Explicitly state what others can reuse.

## 2. Weak or Missing Evaluation
- Problem: Only a demonstration or author opinion; no rigorous test of utility/quality/efficacy.
- Fix: Match evaluation strength to claims. Use multiple methods if claiming strong utility. Report metrics and limitations honestly.

## 3. Invisible Search Process
- Problem: Final artifact appears fully formed; no evidence of iteration, alternatives, or trade-offs.
- Fix: Document key design decisions, rejected alternatives, and how constraints shaped the solution.

## 4. Unclear Artifact Type
- Problem: Reader cannot tell whether the contribution is a model, method, instantiation, or something else.
- Fix: Name the artifact type early and consistently.

## 5. Problem Not Relevant or Poorly Motivated
- Problem: Academic gap exists but practical importance is weak (or vice versa).
- Fix: Combine literature gap analysis with practitioner evidence or real organizational pain.

## 6. Contributions Overclaimed or Underclaimed
- Problem: Claiming a full design theory when only a situated implementation exists, or failing to claim the principles that were actually produced.
- Fix: Use Gregor & Hevner levels accurately. Prefer modest, well-supported claims.

## 7. One-Audience Writing
- Problem: Paper is either too technical (managers cannot see value) or too managerial (technologists cannot reproduce or extend).
- Fix: Dual-layer communication — technical depth in design/evaluation sections, value and implications in introduction and discussion.

## 8. Evaluation Criteria Not Linked to Objectives
- Problem: Metrics appear arbitrary or do not map back to the stated design objectives.
- Fix: Derive evaluation criteria directly from the objectives defined in Phase 1.

## 9. Missing Kernel Theory or Justificatory Knowledge
- Problem: Design decisions appear arbitrary.
- Fix: Ground principles in existing theory, empirical findings, or explicit design experience when possible. Not mandatory, but strengthens rigor.

## 10. Ignoring Boundary Conditions
- Problem: Results presented as universal.
- Fix: Explicitly state the context in which the artifact was evaluated and the conditions under which the principles are expected to hold.
