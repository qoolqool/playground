# Contribution Framing Templates

## Design Principles Template

Use this structure for each design principle:

**Design Principle X: [Short Name]**

- **Statement**: To achieve [goal] in [context], the artifact should [actionable prescription].
- **Rationale**: Grounded in [kernel theory / empirical insight / design experience].
- **Evidence**: Evaluation showed [result] that supports this principle because [link].
- **Boundary conditions**: Applies when [conditions]; may not hold when [exceptions].

## Contribution Statement Patterns

**Artifact contribution**
"This research contributes a novel [type] artifact that [key capability] for [problem class]."

**Knowledge contribution (preferred)**
"This research contributes a set of design principles for [problem class] that [advance existing knowledge by …]. The principles were instantiated in [artifact] and evaluated through [method], demonstrating [outcomes]."

**Methodological contribution**
"This research contributes a [new or refined] method for [task] that improves upon prior approaches by [difference]."

## Gregor & Hevner Positioning

In the introduction or discussion, explicitly position the contribution:

"Following Gregor and Hevner (2013), this work is positioned as a Level 2 contribution — a nascent design theory consisting of [constructs / design principles / method] for the class of problems [description]."

## Linking Evaluation to Principles

Always close the loop:

"Evaluation results provide empirical support for Design Principle 3. In the case study, the [mechanism] led to [measurable improvement], confirming the principle's efficacy under [conditions]."
